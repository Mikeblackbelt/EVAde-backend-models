# Instruments, Regulation, Ethics, and Safety

Status labels: **VERIFIED** = bibliographic record and abstract (or the primary document) checked; **PARTIAL** = record checked, abstract not read or source is secondary; **UNVERIFIED** = not confirmed. Nothing here is legal advice.

## 1. Questionnaires

- **PHQ-9.** Levis, Benedetti & Thombs (2019). *BMJ*, 365, l1476. DOI 10.1136/bmj.l1476. VERIFIED. Individual-participant-data meta-analysis, 58 studies, n = 17,357. At cutoff 10 or higher against semistructured interviews: sensitivity 0.88, specificity 0.85; sensitivity against fully structured interviews was 5-22% lower. A screening flag in general and clinical populations, not validated in ICE settings.
- **PHQ-2 and staged screening.** Levis et al. (2020). *JAMA*, 323(22), 2290-2300. DOI 10.1001/jama.2020.6504. VERIFIED. 100 studies, n = 44,318. PHQ-2 cutoff 2: sensitivity 0.91, specificity 0.67; cutoff 3: 0.72 and 0.85. Staged PHQ-2 then PHQ-9: 0.82 vs 0.86 for PHQ-9 alone.
- **Cutoff selection.** Levis et al. (2024). *JAMA Network Open*, 7(11), e2429630. DOI 10.1001/jamanetworkopen.2024.29630. VERIFIED. Population-optimal cutoff was 8; small studies found optimal cutoffs from 2 to 21 and overestimated sensitivity by 6.4 points. The same group authored these papers, so frame as "cutoff 8-10, validate locally". Do not tune cutoffs on small crew samples.
- **GAD-7.** Two recent meta-analyses disagree. Aktürk et al. (2025), *Cochrane Database Syst Rev*, 3, CD015455, DOI 10.1002/14651858.CD015455 (VERIFIED): 48 studies, n = 19,228; at cutoff 10, sensitivity 0.64 and specificity 0.91 for GAD; few low-risk-of-bias studies. Park & Park (2025), *J Affect Disord*, 391, 119913, DOI 10.1016/j.jad.2025.119913 (VERIFIED): 45 studies, sensitivity 0.81, specificity 0.78, AUC 0.87. The Cochrane figure is more conservative and likely closer to a non-help-seeking crew population. Use GAD-7 as a **symptom tracker (change over time)**, not a detector with a fixed cutoff.
- **Measurement-based care.** Lewis et al. (2019). *JAMA Psychiatry*, 76(3), 324-335. DOI 10.1001/jamapsychiatry.2018.3329. VERIFIED. Narrative review: underused, with patient confidentiality concerns as a barrier. A crew-facing score only helps if it connects to a response pathway.
- **Licensing.** The official PHQ-9 PDF carries the footer "No permission required to reproduce, translate, display or distribute" (text extracted from the PDF; VERIFIED). A 2010 news report of a Pfizer announcement says the PHQ and GAD-7 are available without copyright restriction (secondary; PARTIAL). The current phqscreeners.com Terms of Use could not be retrieved (UNVERIFIED), and some third-party copies carry conflicting copyright notices. Keep the attribution, use unmodified wording, and check the current terms before commercial embedding.
- **What NASA uses.** Per NASA slides retrieved by search (not read in full; PARTIAL), standard measures include POMS, BDI-II, CES-D, and NeuroVAS, plus actigraphy and cognition testing, not PHQ-9. Consider BDI-II and POMS crosswalks for NASA audiences.

## 2. Alertness and cognition

- **Cognition battery.** Basner et al. (2015). *Aerosp Med Hum Perform*, 86(11), 942-952. DOI 10.3357/AMHP.4343.2015. VERIFIED. Ten tests, 15 forms. After one night of total sleep deprivation: vigilant attention d = 1.00, cognitive throughput d = 0.68, abstract reasoning d = 0.65.
- **Practice effects.** Basner et al. (2020). *J Clin Exp Neuropsychol*, 42(5), 516-529. DOI 10.1080/13803395.2020.1773765. VERIFIED. N = 46; protracted nonlinear practice effects beyond the second administration for most tests; correction factors provided. Without correction, improvement can mask real decline.
- **Brief PVT.** Basner, Mollicone & Dinges (2011). *Acta Astronaut*, 69(11-12), 949-959. DOI 10.1016/j.actaastro.2011.07.015. VERIFIED. N = 74; a 70% shorter test lost on average 22.7% of effect size; lowering the lapse threshold from 500 to 355 ms raised effect sizes. Authors call for further applied validation.
- **PVT metrics.** Basner & Dinges (2011). *Sleep*, 34(5), 581-591. DOI 10.1093/sleep/34.5.581. VERIFIED. Lapses and 1/RT-based metrics were the most sensitive.
- **Adaptive PVT-BA.** Basner (2022). *Sleep Advances*, 3(1), zpac038 (erratum 2023, zpad022). DOI 10.1093/sleepadvances/zpac038. VERIFIED. Mean test duration 1 min 43 s; kappa versus PVT-B 0.92 (training) and 0.85 (validation); laboratory data only.
- **Repeated PVT administration.** Basner et al. (2018). *Sleep*, 41(1), zsx187. DOI 10.1093/sleep/zsx187. PARTIAL (abstract not read).
- **ISS cognition.** Dev et al. (2024); see the hub document. No systematic decline over 6-month missions.

## 3. Sleep trackers

- **Lee et al. (2025)** Performance of consumer wrist-worn sleep tracking devices compared to polysomnography: a meta-analysis. *J Clin Sleep Med*, 21(3), 573-582. DOI 10.5664/jcsm.11460. VERIFIED. 24 studies, n = 798; total sleep time difference -16.9 min versus PSG; waking after sleep onset +13.3 min.
- **Schyvens et al. (2024)** Accuracy of Fitbit Charge 4, Garmin Vivosmart 4, and WHOOP versus polysomnography. *JMIR mHealth uHealth*, 12, e52192. DOI 10.2196/52192. VERIFIED. 8 studies; staging accuracy varied by stage.
- **Svensson et al. (2024)** Oura Ring Generation 3 versus ambulatory PSG, 96 participants. *Sleep Med*, DOI 10.1016/j.sleep.2024.01.020. VERIFIED (volume and pages unconfirmed). Sleep-wake sensitivity about 94%, specificity about 73-75%; a published comment challenges aspects of it.
- **Use:** trust total sleep time and efficiency trends more than stage durations; log device model and firmware.
- **Wearable sleep and depression.** Zhang et al. (2021). *JMIR mHealth uHealth*, 9(4), e24604. DOI 10.2196/24604. VERIFIED. 2,812 PHQ-8 records from 368 participants; several sleep features associated with symptom severity. Moshe et al. (2021). *Frontiers in Psychiatry*, 12, 625247. DOI 10.3389/fpsyt.2021.625247. VERIFIED. n = 60 over 2 weeks; exploratory, p-values near .02-.04 with no stated multiplicity correction. Associations, not individual-level prediction.

## 4. Digital phenotyping and passive sensing

- **Leaning et al. (2024)** *Neurosci Biobehav Rev*, 158, 105541. DOI 10.1016/j.neubiorev.2024.105541. VERIFIED. 24 studies, 9,801 participants; moderate performance; lack of external validation.
- **Choi, Ooi & Lottridge (2024)** *JMIR mHealth uHealth*, 12, e40689. DOI 10.2196/40689. VERIFIED. 36 papers; context matters. A habitat restricts mobility by design, so location features need re-baselining.
- **Xu et al. (2022)** GLOBEM dataset. *NeurIPS 2022 Datasets and Benchmarks*. DOI 10.52202/068431-1790. VERIFIED. Over 700 user-years from 497 users; 18 algorithms benchmarked; cross-dataset generalization "needs further research".
- **Adler et al. (2022)** Generalization across longitudinal mobile sensing studies. *PLoS ONE*, 17(4), e0266516. DOI 10.1371/journal.pone.0266516. VERIFIED. Combined-data models beat a training-mean baseline more often than single-study models; modest effects.
- **Henson et al. (2021)** Anomaly detection to predict relapse risk in schizophrenia. *Transl Psychiatry*, 11, 28. DOI 10.1038/s41398-020-01123-7. VERIFIED. 89% sensitivity, 75% specificity from individualized baselines. Small clinical cohort; expect many false alarms.
- **Onnela & Rauch (2016)** *Neuropsychopharmacology*, 41(7), 1691-1696. DOI 10.1038/npp.2016.7. VERIFIED. Active versus passive data.

## 5. Regulation (context, not advice)

- **FDA general wellness guidance.** CDRH, *General Wellness: Policy for Low Risk Devices*, issued 6 Jan 2026, superseding the 2019 version (primary PDF text extracted; VERIFIED). Examples of wellness claims include stress management, mental acuity, and sleep management without reference to a disease. A claim that a product helps treat an anxiety disorder is outside the category. Products should not guide specific clinical action or display clinical-style values unless validated.
- **FDA clinical decision support software guidance.** January 2026 (FDA page read, guidance body not read; PARTIAL). A crew-facing risk score is unlikely to meet the non-device CDS criteria, which need a clinician user and transparency. This is an inference to confirm with regulatory counsel.
- **FDA pulse oximeter draft guidance.** Issued 7 Jan 2025; no sign of finalization was found; see [vital-sign-monitoring](../vital-sign-monitoring/README.md).
- **EU AI Act.** From the European Commission AI Act Service Desk timeline (VERIFIED for these dates): entry into force 1 Aug 2024; prohibitions 2 Feb 2025; most rules 2 Aug 2026; after the Digital Omnibus amendments, Annex III high-risk rules apply 2 Dec 2027 and Annex I (AI in regulated products, including medical devices) 2 Aug 2028. Article 5(1)(f) text from a non-official mirror (PARTIAL) prohibits inferring emotions in workplace and education settings except for medical or safety reasons. Confirm the Official Journal references on EUR-Lex.
- **Implication.** Depression or anxiety detection, severity scoring, and crisis prompts move a product toward device regulation. Stress and sleep trend monitoring sits closer to wellness. Decide intended use first.

## 6. Ethics and privacy

- **Reed & Antonsen (2018)** *AMA J Ethics*, 20(9), E849-856. DOI 10.1001/amajethics.2018.849. PARTIAL. An agency acts as employer, clinician, and research sponsor at once; astronaut privacy is hard to protect in a small, visible cohort.
- **National Academies / IOM (2014)** *Health Standards for Long Duration and Exploration Spaceflight: Ethics Principles, Responsibilities, and Decision Framework.* DOI 10.17226/18576. PARTIAL (title verified; text not read).
- **Martinez-Martin et al. (2018)** Data mining for health: staking out the ethical territory of digital phenotyping. *npj Digit Med*, 1, 68. DOI 10.1038/s41746-018-0075-8. PARTIAL.
- **Design implications.** Separate research and operational data flows, give crew control over what is shared, plan for re-identification risk in small cohorts, define retention and deletion rules, and obtain institutional review (IRB or equivalent) for any human-subjects data collection.

## 7. Suicide risk and safety escalation

- **Belsher et al. (2019)** Prediction models for suicide attempts and deaths: a systematic review and simulation. *JAMA Psychiatry*, 76(6), 642-651. DOI 10.1001/jamapsychiatry.2019.0174. VERIFIED. 17 cohorts, 64 models, over 14 million participants; most had global accuracy at or above 0.80, but positive predictive value for suicide death was at or below 0.01. The authors say models are not ready for clinical application. EHR-based, not wearable.
- **Implication.** Never present a passive-sensor score as a suicide predictor. Escalate on explicit self-report (for example a positive response on a suicidal-ideation item, or direct disclosure) and human judgment under a documented crisis protocol. No PHQ-9 item-9 predictive-validity paper was verified, so no performance claim is made.

## 8. Implementation steps

1. Write the intended-use statement and get regulatory review before building features.
2. Use PHQ-2 as a frequent gate and PHQ-9 and GAD-7 as periodic trackers; pre-register cutoffs; do not tune them on small samples.
3. Keep original item wording and attribution; confirm current terms before commercial release.
4. Build the cognition and alertness module on PVT-B or PVT-BA with practice-effect handling; baseline each person pre-mission.
5. Log sleep-device model and firmware; report device-versus-PSG error with any sleep claim.
6. Use within-person baselines and anomaly detection; route all flags through human review.
7. Evaluate with leave-one-person-out and cross-cohort tests and a trivial baseline.
8. Write the safety-escalation protocol (named clinician, response-time target) before the first participant.
9. Write the data-governance plan (separate flows, small-cohort safeguards, consent, retention).
10. Track EU AI Act dates if EU exposure is possible.

## 9. Open questions

- Is the intended user the individual (wellness) or an operations and medical team (clinical)?
- Which reference standard labels "behavioral health state" in crews? No ICE-validated cutoff exists for any instrument.
- Do the missing 2020+ Mars-500, NEK, LunAres, and HI-SEAS results exist behind paywalls? A targeted database search is needed before claiming absence.
